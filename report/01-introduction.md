# Introduction

My aim is to grow wealth over many years and still be able to leave it alone when markets fall. I do not treat the money as one pile with one appetite for risk. I treat it as a **managed portfolio** with three blocks, and each block has one job.

| Block | Job | What it holds | Currency | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Safety | Pay ordinary life without forced sales | Cash and short-term bonds | Reais | Twelve months of spending |
| Inflation | Fund spending that already has a date | NTN-B (Tesouro IPCA+), with maturities matched to those dates | Reais | Up to about 60 percent |
| Growth | Grow wealth over the long run | A fund of ETFs | The currency of each ETF | 25 to 40 percent |

**Safety.** Twelve months of spending sit in cash and short-term bonds that can be reached in a few days. The return is not the point. This block turns a bad year in the market into a number on a screen, instead of a sale I am forced to make.

**Inflation.** Some future spending already has a date: retirement income, or a bill I can already name. A bond that pays a fixed amount can lose to rising prices. NTN-B pays inflation plus a real rate, in reais, and its maturity can match the date of the expense. The block is capped so that protecting the long run does not consume the portfolio.

**Growth.** This is money I can allow to fall, because no dated bill depends on it. It is equity, so it carries the most risk and the most expected return. It is also the block this course builds.

The first two blocks live in reais, the currency I spend. They keep spending and savings in the same currency. The growth block is different: it holds ETFs in their own currencies, and the mix of currencies is part of its diversification.

## The growth block is a fund of ETFs

The growth block is an actively managed **fund of ETFs**. It is not tied to an index. A rule decides which ETFs it holds and what weight each one gets. The research question of this project is that rule: which ETFs earn a place, in what weight, so that the growth block compounds without breaking the first two blocks.

I do not pick companies. S&P Dow Jones Indices, in the SPIVA U.S. Scorecard for year-end 2024, found that about 84 percent of active large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 over ten years, and more than 90 percent over fifteen. An ETF already gives a diversified basket at a low cost. The open decision is which ETFs, and in what weight.

## Why this is worth studying

The three blocks are already chosen. The weights inside the growth block are not. The users are investors like me: people who spend in reais, keep a safety cushion and dated goals, and want the rest of their wealth to grow worldwide without picking stocks. I am the first. The application is the program that runs the rule for each of them.
